How it works

It sits alongside your check-in system. Your key fobs and front desk keep working exactly as they do now.

  1. 1

    A small computer reads your camera and your desk

    A Raspberry Pi sits by your camera recorder. A small splitter copies your front-desk screen to it, so it sees each scan with nothing installed on your computers. Nothing is wired into your door.

  2. 2

    It counts who came through

    Only when something moves at the door, and only during your hours. Each entry is matched with a scan at the desk. People leaving and people waiting in line are left alone.

  3. 3

    You get the short list

    An alert on your phone or by email when something's off, with the photo. In the morning, your gym page shows last night with the problems first.

The first two weeks

Most of what makes it accurate is the camera, not the software.

  1. 1

    Check the angle

    A camera high up, looking down at the turnstile, works best: from above, two people close together are still two heads.

  2. 2

    Check the light

    Glass doors with headlights or sun behind them cause the most mistakes. Look at a few night photos in week one.

  3. 3

    Run it quietly first

    For the first week, compare flags with what really happened before anyone acts on them. Mark each one.

  4. 4

    Tell your front desk

    A flag means "check the photo", not "confront a member". Two minutes of training avoids most awkward moments.

Questions

Is this facial recognition?

No. SecurityAI counts people and describes what they did at your door. It doesn't identify anyone, and it can't tell you whether a specific person is a member, an employee or a stranger.

What gets installed at my gym?

A Raspberry Pi (about the size of a deck of cards) on the same network as your camera recorder, and a small HDMI splitter behind your front-desk screen so it can see each check-in. Nothing is installed on your computers, and nothing touches your door or your check-in system. It works with any check-in system: a pop-up, a list of check-ins or a screen that only shows names. With an iPad at the desk, an adapter replaces the splitter.

What if it gets it wrong?

It will, sometimes: bad light, an odd angle or a crowded doorway can cause a false flag or a miss. Treat every flag as "worth a look", not a verdict. That's why every flag comes with the photos, and a weekly two-minute spot-check tells you if it's missing anyone.

Does it replace my front desk or key fobs?

No. If your internet or the camera computer goes down, your access control keeps working; you'd just stop getting flags until it's back, and we'd be told.

How long does setup take?

An afternoon on site with you, in the room where your camera recorder is. Then plan on a week of quietly comparing flags with what really happened before you rely on it.

What happens to the photos?

No video is stored. Photos of each entry are kept 48 hours so you can check them, then deleted. Flagged entries are kept 35 days for your monthly report. Your recorder keeps its own recording, as it does now.

Can I add a second gym later?

Yes. Each location gets its own camera computer, its own gym page and its own alert list, for $100 a month more.
