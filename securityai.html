HOW IT WORKS

Three steps, no new hardware

SecurityAI sits alongside your existing check-in system. It doesn't replace your key fobs or front desk — it watches the door and tells you when someone slips past them.

01

Point a camera at the door

Use the camera already mounted over your entrance, or a $60 IP camera. No turnstile or new access hardware required.

02

Claude watches the feed

Motion at the doorway triggers a short burst of frames, which Claude reads together as a sequence and counts how many people actually crossed. You tell it how many to expect at that door; more than that is a flag. Nothing is analyzed while the door sits empty.

03

Front desk gets the alert

An unmatched entry pushes a notification to the front desk screen, a manager's phone, or Slack — with a timestamp and camera name, in seconds.

WHAT IT DOES

Built around one job

Works with cameras you already have

If your cameras are already up on a screen somewhere — an NVR wall, a security app, a browser dashboard — SecurityAI reads that. No new cameras, no access-control wiring, nothing mounted.

Flags more people than expected

You set how many people should come through a door at once — usually one. If a burst shows more, that's an alert, with a plain-language note and the frame that triggered it so you can check for yourself.

Tells a queue from a violation

People standing near the door waiting their turn to scan aren't the same as people entering unscanned. SecurityAI separates the two so a busy front desk doesn't turn into a wall of false alerts.

Understands your accessible gate

If your entrance has a separate accessible or disabled-access gate, tell SecurityAI once — it stops treating that gate's unmatched scans as violations and instead logs them for a manual check-in match, since accessible gates are often wired outside the main scan hardware.

Alerts with a picture attached

Nobody is expected to watch a camera wall overnight. Alerts go out by email and SMS only when something needs a look — and every one keeps the frame that set it off, so staff can confirm it rather than take it on faith.

A log of flags, not a library of footage

The searchable record is entry events — time, camera, outcome — not stored video, which keeps the system out of the business of archiving footage of members.

NOTE

Cameras pointed at an entrance are subject to local surveillance and signage laws. Before installing, check your state and municipal requirements for posted notice, retention limits, and employee/member consent — these vary widely and SecurityAI doesn't handle legal compliance on your behalf.

WHY THIS APPROACH

What's actually different here

Most tools in this space fall into two camps: dedicated hardware sensors mounted at the door, or software tied to a fixed, trained detection model. This sits in a third spot, and it's worth being specific about the real trade-off rather than just claiming it's better.

Watches for movement, not on a stopwatch

Nothing is analyzed while a doorway sits empty. Local motion detection runs constantly on just the entrance regions — costing nothing — and only when something actually moves does a short burst of frames get analyzed as a sequence. That means the analysis starts the moment an event begins rather than whenever the next scheduled check happens to land, and a fast entry gets read across several frames instead of one frozen instant.

Rules in English, not retraining

"Ignore the accessible gate," "these zones should be empty right now," "a queue isn't a violation" — each of those is a sentence in a prompt here, not a retraining job. A fixed classifier can do the same job once it's been trained for it; changing what counts as a violation on this system is an edit, not a model update.

A reason, not just a score

Every result comes with a plain-language note — what was actually seen, not just a confidence number. That's what makes it possible to tell a false flag from a real one at a glance in the alert log, instead of pulling video for every single alert.

You own the code, not a subscription to a black box

This is source you can read, self-host, and modify — not a closed platform you're locked into. That cuts both ways: it also means you're responsible for hosting it and keeping it running, which a managed SaaS product would otherwise handle for you.

This is a genuine trade-off, not a universal upgrade — a mature, trained-model product from a team that's been doing this for gyms specifically has real advantages too: proven accuracy at scale, and features like automated billing for guest fees that this doesn't have (yet). Worth comparing directly against whatever else you're evaluating before deciding.

GOING LIVE

Putting this on your actual door

The demo above proves the concept. Moving it to a real entrance is a short, physical checklist — most of it is about the camera, not the software.

01

Mount at chest height

Aim it straight at the doorway, not down from the ceiling — a steep angle makes it hard to tell one person from two standing close together.

02

Check the light

Test at your darkest hour (early morning open, late close) and your brightest (a sunlit entrance at noon). Backlighting from glass doors is the most common failure mode.

03

Run it in the background first

Let it flag entries for a week without telling staff to act on it. Compare the flags against what actually happened before you trust it operationally.

04

Tell your front desk what a flag means

A flag means "check the log," not "confront a member." Two minutes of training avoids most awkward moments.

WHAT'S NEXT

Where this is headed

Being straight about what exists today versus what's planned — everything above this line is built and working; everything below isn't yet.

A phone app

The obvious next step, and the one most gym owners will want: alerts as push notifications instead of email, and a feed you can scroll through in the morning to see what happened overnight. The pieces are already in place for it — the server keeps a real alert log and already sends SMS — so an app would mostly be a nicer window onto data that exists. Not built yet.

History beyond 24 hours

The alert log now holds a rolling 24 hours and survives restarts, which covers "what happened last night." What it isn't yet is an archive — there's no way to pull up last month, filter by date, or export to a spreadsheet. That's the next thing to build, and it's what turns a nightly check into evidence you can act on over time.

Recovering the money, not just reporting it

Right now SecurityAI tells you when and how often unscanned entries happen. It can't tell you who, by design — that would mean facial recognition, which carries real legal exposure under biometric privacy laws in several states. The realistic version is tighter integration with your check-in system so a flagged entry lands next to the member record your staff already has, and they make the call.

Tuning from real footage

The motion thresholds are reasoned starting points, tested against simulated movement rather than a real gym doorway at 2am. Every real deployment makes them better. If you're an early customer, expect some tuning in the first weeks — and expect to be asked what it got wrong.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Before you set it up

Is this facial recognition?

No. SecurityAI counts people and reads behavior at your door — it doesn't identify who anyone is, and it can't tell you whether a specific person is a member, an employee, or a stranger. If two people look similar, it has no way to know if they're the same person or not.

What if it gets it wrong?

It will, sometimes — bad lighting, an unusual angle, or a crowded doorway can all produce a false flag or miss a real one. Treat every flag as "worth a look," not a verdict. That's why the going-live checklist above recommends a background-only trial period before anyone acts on an alert.

Does it replace my front desk or key fobs?

No — it sits alongside whatever check-in system you already use and watches for entries that don't match it. If your internet or camera goes down, your existing access control keeps working; you'd just stop getting flags until it's back.

How long does setup actually take?

For the single-frame demo on this page: a couple of minutes, using a webcam or a screen share of your existing camera dashboard. Wiring it into a real entrance with a dedicated camera and a review process is closer to the going-live checklist above — plan on a week of background testing before relying on it.

Can I add a second gym later?

Yes — moving from the single-gym plan to the multi-gym contract rate is a plan change, not a new setup from scratch. Each location still gets its own cameras and its own alert log.

What happens to the footage?

Individual frames are analyzed and then discarded — what's retained is the alert log itself (timestamps, counts, outcomes), not stored video. You're still responsible for whatever your camera's own recording system does separately, and for any local signage or consent laws that apply to it.
