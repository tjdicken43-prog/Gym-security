HOW IT WORKS

Three steps, no new cameras

SecurityAI sits alongside your check-in system. It doesn't replace your key fobs or front desk. It watches the door and tells you when more people came through than you expect.

01

A small computer reads your entrance camera

A laptop or a Raspberry Pi sits next to your camera recorder in the back room and watches the one camera that covers your door. Nothing new is mounted and nothing is wired into your door.

02

Claude counts who crossed

When something moves in the doorway, a few photos of that moment are sent to Claude, which counts how many people actually came through. You say how many to expect at that door (usually one). More than that is a flag. Nothing is analyzed while the door is empty.

03

You get the photo

A flag goes out by email or text message to whoever you choose, with the photo and the time. In the morning, your gym's activity page lists everything from overnight, flagged entries first.

WHAT IT DOES

Built around one job

Works with cameras you already have

It reads your entrance camera from the recorder (NVR) your cameras already feed. No new cameras and no access-control wiring. The only addition is a small computer beside the recorder. We check your recorder works with it during setup.

Flags more people than expected

You set how many people should come through the door at once, usually one. If more cross, you get a flag with a plain-language note of what was seen and the photos, so you can check for yourself.

Tells a queue from a problem

People waiting near the door for their turn aren't the same as extra people coming through. SecurityAI tells the two apart so a busy doorway doesn't turn into a wall of false alerts.

Understands your accessible gate

If your entrance has a separate accessible gate, tell us once. An extra person there isn't treated as tailgating; it's listed for you to match against your check-in log instead.

Photos kept briefly, not a video archive

No video is stored. Photos of each crossing are deleted after 48 hours. Entries flagged as tailgating are kept for 35 days so they can go in your monthly report.

NOTE

Cameras pointed at an entrance are subject to local surveillance and signage laws. Check your state and city requirements for posted notice and member/employee consent. SecurityAI doesn't handle legal compliance for you.

WHY THIS APPROACH

What's actually different here

Most tools in this space are either dedicated sensors mounted at the door or software tied to a fixed, trained model. This sits in a third spot. Here's the honest trade-off.

Watches for movement, not on a timer

Motion detection runs constantly on the doorway alone, on the computer at your gym. Only when something moves are a few photos of that moment analyzed together, so a fast entry is read across several photos instead of one frozen instant.

A reason, not just a score

Every flag comes with a plain-language note of what was seen, like "a second person follows close behind without stopping at the reader." That's what lets you tell a false flag from a real one at a glance.

A hosted service, run by a small team

We host the website, the analysis and the alerts, and handle updates. You don't run any software yourself. It's a small operation, so support is by email and phone during business hours.

A mature, trained-model product built for gyms has real advantages too: proven accuracy at scale, and features like automated guest-fee billing that this doesn't have. Worth comparing before you decide.

THE FIRST TWO WEEKS

Getting it right on your door

Most of what makes it accurate is about the camera, not the software.

01

Check the angle

A camera mounted high, looking down at the gate or turnstile, works best: from above, two people close together are still two separate heads. A low camera looking along the line of people can hide the second person behind the first.

02

Check the light

Glass doors with headlights or sun behind them are the most common cause of mistakes. Look at a few night-time photos in the first week.

03

Run it quietly first

For the first week, compare the flags against what really happened before anyone acts on them. Mark each one "Tailgate" or "It was fine".

04

Tell your front desk

A flag means "check the photo," not "confront a member." Two minutes of training avoids most awkward moments.

WHAT'S NEXT

What exists, and what doesn't yet

Everything above this line is built and working. Everything below isn't yet.

Push notifications

Today, alerts come by email or text, and your activity page is the morning feed: it opens on any phone and can be added to the home screen. A native app with push notifications isn't built.

History beyond 48 hours

The activity page covers "what happened last night," and flagged entries are kept 35 days for the monthly report. There's no searchable archive, date filter or spreadsheet export yet.

Matching to your check-in system

SecurityAI can tell you when and how often more people came through than expected. It can't tell you who, by design: that would mean facial recognition. The realistic next step is putting a flagged entry next to your check-in records so your staff make the call.

Tuning from real doors

The motion settings are reasoned starting points. If you're an early customer, expect some tuning in the first weeks, and expect to be asked what it got wrong.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Before you sign up

Is this facial recognition?

No. SecurityAI counts people and describes what they did at your door. It doesn't identify anyone, and it can't tell you whether a specific person is a member, an employee or a stranger.

What gets installed at my gym?

A laptop or a Raspberry Pi (a small computer about the size of a deck of cards), plugged into power and into the same network as your camera recorder. It needs an internet connection. Nothing is mounted and nothing touches your door or check-in system.

What if it gets it wrong?

It will, sometimes: bad lighting, an odd angle or a crowded doorway can cause a false flag or a miss. Treat every flag as "worth a look," not a verdict. That's why every flag comes with the photos.

Does it replace my front desk or key fobs?

No. It sits alongside your check-in system. If your internet or the camera computer goes down, your access control keeps working; you'd just stop getting flags until it's back.

How long does setup take?

We set it up with you on site, in the room where your camera recorder is. Then plan on a week of quietly comparing flags against what really happened before you rely on it.

What happens to the photos?

No video is stored. Photos of each crossing are kept for 48 hours so you can check them on your activity page, then deleted. Entries flagged as tailgating are kept for 35 days for your monthly report. Your camera recorder keeps doing its own recording separately, as it does now.

Can I add a second gym later?

Yes. Each location gets its own camera computer, its own activity page and its own alert list.
